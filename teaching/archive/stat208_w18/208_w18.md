# STATS 208: Statistical Learning Theory
## Winter 2018
The course provides an introduction to the theoretical analysis of machine learning methods,
with an emphasis on prediction problems. Both the statistical and the computational aspects
of the problems will be considered. The course covers approaches such as kernel methods,
convex optimization based approaches, ensemble methods, neural networks, as well as online
approaches. The theory of kernel methods will be covered in some depth. Time-permitting
topics from unsupervised learning such as community detection in networks and spectral
clustering will be discussed.

## Tentative Syllabus
- Probabilistic framework for prediction problems
- Empirical risk minimization, perceptron algorithm, neural nets
- Risk bounds: Rademacher averages and VC dimension.
- Classification: discriminant analysis, convex surrogate losses, Support Vector Machines (SVM).
- Kernel methods and Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS).
- Online methods: stochastic gradient, mirror descent.
- Ensemble methods: boosting.
- Community detection and spectral clustering.


## Recommended Books
There is no official text for the course, but you might find the following books useful:
- T. Hastie, R. Tibshirani and J. Friedman. The Elements of Statistical Learning. 2nd
Ed., Springer, 2009.
- M. Wainwright, High-dimensional statistics: A Non-asymptotic Viewpoint.
(Chapters from the draft will be posted.)

- Cucker, Felipe, and Ding Xuan Zhou. Learning theory: an approximation theory view-
point. Vol. 24. Cambridge University Press, 2007.
- Vladimir Koltchinskii, Oracle Inequalities in Empirical Risk Minimization and Sparse
Recovery Problems.

- Evarist Giné and Richard Nickl, [*Mathematical Foundations of Infinite-Dimensional Statistical Models*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/mathematical-foundations-of-infinitedimensional-statistical-models/C9731BF27A4CDBDB297404EBF1B7820E).

## Prerequisites
STATS 200A and 200B (or graduate level probability theory and theoretical statistics) and preferably 200C. Also, [*mathematical maturity*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_maturity).

## Grading
The grade will be based 50% on homework and 50% on the final project.
- *Homework*. Problems sets will be assigned roughly every two weeks. Students are encouraged to discuss
the problems together, but must independently produce and submit solutions. Use of LaTeX in preparing the solutions is strongly encouraged.
- *Project*. The final project can be in any area related to the course. You might extend a
theoretical result, summarize existing results (a survey paper), develop a new method or run
experiments on existing methods, or do a combination of these. You must submit a brief
written report and give a presentation in class during the last week. You can work on the
project in groups of two.
